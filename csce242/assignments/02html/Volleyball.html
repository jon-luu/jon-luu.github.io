How LEGO Started

From a Carpenter's Workshop to a Global Toy

The Lego Group was founded in 1932 by Ole Kirk Christiansen, a carpenter in Billund, Denmark, who began making wooden toys after his carpentry business struggled during the Great Depression. In 1934, he named the company "Lego," a shortened combination of the Danish words "leg godt," meaning "play well." For its first two decades, the company made a variety of wooden toys, and it wasn't until 1947 that Lego bought a plastic injection molding machine, one of the first companies in Denmark to do so, which opened the door to the plastic building blocks the brand would later become known for.

The modern interlocking brick, with its stud-and-tube design, was patented by Lego in 1958. That single design decision is what let bricks made decades apart still click together, and it became the foundation the entire company was built on going forward. Ole Kirk's son, Godtfred Kirk Christiansen, pushed the idea further by developing the "System of Play," the philosophy that every Lego set should work with every other Lego set. That single choice, made over sixty years ago, is still the reason a set bought today can be combined with one from a toy box in the attic.
